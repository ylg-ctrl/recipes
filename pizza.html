Margherita Pizza

Description

Margherita pizza is the quintessential classic Neapolitan pizza, known for its simple yet harmonious combination of sweet crushed tomatoes, creamy fresh mozzarella cheese, and fragrant basil leaves. It represents the colors of the Italian flag and delivers authentic artisanal pizzeria flavor right from your home oven.

Using high-heat baking and a preheated pizza stone or baking steel creates a wonderfully crisp, blistered crust with a light and airy interior. It is simple to make and endlessly satisfying.

Ingredients

  • 1 ball (approx. 250g) homemade or store-bought pizza dough
  • 1/2 cup canned San Marzano whole peeled tomatoes, crushed by hand
  • 6 oz fresh mozzarella cheese, sliced or torn into pieces
  • Handful of fresh basil leaves
  • 2 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine sea salt
  • 1/4 cup semolina flour or cornmeal for dusting

Steps

  1. Place a pizza stone or heavy baking sheet on the upper-middle rack of your oven and preheat to 500°F (260°C) or highest temperature for at least 45 minutes.
  2. Bring pizza dough to room temperature by letting it rest on a floured surface covered with a damp towel for 30 to 60 minutes.
  3. In a small bowl, crush San Marzano tomatoes with a fork or by hand, stirring in a pinch of sea salt and a splash of olive oil.
  4. Dust a pizza peel or parchment sheet with semolina flour. Gently stretch and press the dough out with your hands from the center outward, forming a 10 to 12-inch round with a slightly raised border.
  5. Evenly spread crushed tomatoes across the dough, leaving a 1/2-inch border around the edge.
  6. Distribute fresh mozzarella pieces evenly over the tomato sauce and drizzle lightly with extra virgin olive oil.
  7. Carefully transfer the pizza onto the preheated stone or baking sheet. Bake for 8 to 10 minutes until the crust is blistered and golden brown and the cheese is melted and bubbling.
  8. Remove the pizza from the oven, garnish immediately with fresh basil leaves, slice, and enjoy hot.
